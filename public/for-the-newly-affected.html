A Gentle Starting Point

If the Stroke Was Recent

This is a frightening, disorienting time — for the person who had the stroke, and for everyone around them. You don't need to take in much right now. This page is short on purpose.

If you're reading this in the first days or weeks after a stroke, the most important things are already being handled by the people caring for you: the medical team, the early rehab, the rest your brain needs to begin healing. Nothing on this website is more important than those.

You don't need any of this yet

This site offers a guided imagery programme — a way of using your mind to support your recovery. It will still be here in a week, a month, a year. There is no rush to start, and starting late costs you nothing. If today all you can manage is rest, then rest is exactly the right thing to be doing.

The one thing worth knowing early

Guided imagery is something you can begin even if you can't move the affected part yet. Imagining a movement uses your brain, not the limb — so it's one of the few active things available to you very early on, when other exercise may not be. But there is still no hurry. Come to it when you're ready.

A few gentle truths for the early days

  • Recovery is not a straight line. Good days and hard days sit side by side. A hard day is not a step backwards.
  • The brain changes fastest in the first few months — but it keeps changing for years. Progress does not stop when the early phase ends.
  • Deep tiredness after a stroke is real and physical. It is not laziness, and resting is part of getting better, not the opposite of it.
  • What you feel able to do will shift week to week. Meet yourself where you are, not where you think you should be.

When you're ready — and only then

There's no test to pass first. When you feel like looking, two gentle starting points:

  • A short, private check that shows you how you imagine — some people see pictures, some feel movement, and it helps to know your own way in.
  • The guided imagery programme itself, which you can read at your own pace.

If someone close to you is helping with your care, the page for family and friends may be useful for them.

See the programme How do you imagine?

If you are in crisis or worried about your immediate health, this website is not the right place — please contact your medical team or local emergency services.
