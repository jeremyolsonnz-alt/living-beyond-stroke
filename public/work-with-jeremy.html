Working Together

Work with Jeremy

The Guided Imagery Programme is free and self-directed, and it always will be. But some people want a guide beside them as they begin. If that's you, there's a small circle you can join, or a quieter one-to-one option, from wherever you are.

"The practices are designed to be used alone. But the first weeks can be the hardest, and having someone to work through them with can be the thing that helps you keep going."

Everything on this website stays free. The audio, the scripts, the reading behind it all — none of that changes. What I offer here is different: my time, in a small group circle or one to one, for anyone who wants a more personal way in.

Jeremy, developer of the Guided Imagery Programme

The Practice Circle

The Practice Circle is a small, recurring group session held over Zoom, open to anyone, anywhere. It's one unhurried hour: a guided practice together, live, followed by conversation. Cameras on or off, whichever you're comfortable with. There's no obligation to speak, and no one is ever put on the spot. For a lot of people, doing this alongside others — even quietly, even off-camera — is part of what makes it work.

These sessions are personal support for living well after stroke. They are not a medical treatment, and they are not a promise of physical recovery — the same honest framing that runs through the whole programme. They sit alongside your rehabilitation team, never in place of it.

One to one — when something is more personal

For some practices and questions, a group isn't the right setting. The quieter one-to-one option is for anything that feels too personal to bring to the circle — continence, swallowing difficulty, speech and communication changes, or intimacy and sexual function after stroke, as examples. Just the two of us, at your pace, for whatever doesn't fit anywhere else.

What we might work on
  • Settling into a regular rhythm with the core practices
  • Choosing which of the five practices fit where you are right now
  • Adapting the imagery so it feels true to you, not borrowed
  • Working through the ordinary blocks: impatience, discomfort, the sense that nothing is happening
  • Learning to notice and use the daily rest windows the programme describes
  • Being guided through a full practice live, at your own pace
Before we meet (optional)

If you'd like a gentle way in before your first session, spend a little time with one of the free practices — whichever one speaks to where you are. There's nothing to prepare and nothing to get right. Noticing how a practice sits with you, unhurried and in your own time, tends to give us a truer starting point than anything we could piece together in the first few minutes of a call.

It isn't a test, and there are no right answers. Whatever you notice — including "nothing much happened" — is useful, and something we'll simply talk through together. If you'd rather come cold, that's completely fine too; we'll find your bearings in the hour itself.

A note on koha

Koha is a Māori custom of voluntary giving: you give what feels right to you, in response to what you've received.

There's no set price here, and no suggested amount. Give what feels right, give what you can, or give nothing at all — that's genuinely fine, and no one will ever know the difference. The circle and the free practices on this site are offered the same way either way.

About Jeremy

I'm the developer of Living Beyond Stroke, a free resource built around two pillars: the science of neuroplasticity and motor imagery, and a Guided Imagery Programme adapted for life after stroke.

I hold a Master of Social Sciences majoring in Psychology from Waikato University, and a Diploma of Solution-Oriented Hypnosis, which I completed with Dr Robert McNeilly at Ampersand in Melbourne in 1998. My graduate research into quality of life after spinal cord injury was carried out under Professor Robert Cummins at Deakin University in Melbourne. The guided imagery approach is something I've been developing and refining for around thirty years.

Dr McNeilly is a medical doctor who studied directly with Milton Erickson, the physician widely regarded as the founder of modern clinical hypnosis. He brought the solution-oriented approach to Australia and taught it with a simple emphasis I've carried ever since: that the person already holds the resources they need, and the work is to help them find their own way to them. That's the spirit these sessions are offered in — gentle, respectful, and led by you rather than done to you.

I haven't had a stroke myself. My work has always been alongside the people who have, as a researcher and a guide rather than someone handing down a treatment.
