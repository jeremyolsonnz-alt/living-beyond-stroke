For Rehabilitation Professionals

For Clinicians

A brief, honest overview of this programme for physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech-language therapists, and other members of the rehabilitation team.

Living Beyond Stroke is a free, self-directed guided imagery programme intended as an adjunct to conventional rehabilitation — not a replacement for it. It applies motor imagery and action observation within a structured framework a person can use independently between therapy sessions, or during periods when hands-on rehab is unavailable.

The evidence base, briefly

The strongest support for motor imagery after stroke is in upper-limb recovery, where several RCTs and systematic reviews report benefit when imagery is combined with physical practice. Evidence for gait is positive but with smaller effect sizes; evidence for balance is more modest; and for speech and language it is genuinely preliminary. The programme's evidence badges reflect this gradient honestly, and the research page and bibliography set out the sources, including where findings are mixed.

The three-phase structure

  • Opening (breath and light): down-regulation to a receptive state before rehearsal.
  • Phase I (the Ideal Being): action observation of the target movement performed with ease, then a dissociation-to-association shift — the observed figure "steps into" the person — moving from third-person observation to first-person kinaesthetic imagery.
  • Phase II (the anatomical pathway): PETTLEP-informed, anatomically specific rehearsal tracing the motor pathway relevant to each practice, paired with a gentle movement intention.
  • Closing: brief rest to support consolidation.

Integrating it with your treatment

  • Pair imagery with physical practice rather than using it alone — the combination outperforms either in isolation.
  • Typical dose in the literature is roughly 3–5 sessions per week; sessions here are capped at around 20 minutes to protect adherence and limit mental fatigue.
  • Screen imagery ability first. The site includes a plain-language imagery self-check built on KVIQ principles; for formal assessment, administer the KVIQ itself.
  • Encourage the observe → imagine → attempt sequence, and real-time (non-accelerated) imagery.
What it is not

It is not a validated combined protocol — the three-phase structure is the author's synthesis, grounded in the imagery literature but not itself trialled as a unit. It is not a substitute for physiotherapy, occupational therapy, or speech-language pathology. And it relies on self-report, with the usual limitations. Please frame it to your patients accordingly.

If you'd like to discuss using this with your service, or have feedback on the clinical framing, the contact page reaches the author directly.
